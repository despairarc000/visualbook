# Research notes: Juan, Pedro, and related Filipino story traditions

**Purpose:** Separate attested collected tales from a newly written continuous Juan-and-Pedro serial. Research checked 2026-10-25. **Completeness disclaimer:** Oral traditions vary by teller, language, locality and period. A finite universal list of every Juan-and-Pedro joke or every character in a single canonical world **does not exist in the surveyed references**. The catalog below covers representative primary collections, documented Juan/Pedro combinations, and modern adaptations; it is *not* an exhaustive worldwide folktale index.

## High-confidence foundations

- **Dean S. Fansler, *Filipino Popular Tales* (1921), Project Gutenberg #8299**, a collection of multiple narrative cycles documented from informants 1908–1914. Fansler explicitly writes that the tradition varies across languages and between tellers. Juan/Pedro appear in unrelated combinations, not one continuous universal chronology. Public source: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/8299 and readable text https://dev.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8299/pg8299-images.html.
- **Mabel Cook Cole, *Philippine Folk Tales* (1916), Project Gutenberg #12814**, includes *The Adventures of Juan* and *Juan Gathers Guavas* and regional tales with different characters/worlds. https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/12814.
- **NCCA's cultural description of *púsong*** places Juan/Suan, Juan Osong, Juan Pusong, and Pilandok among related regional trickster forms, often challenging powerful authority through wit. This is a **typological relationship**, not proof they are the same fictional person. https://www.flickr.com/photos/nccaofficial/18624724062/ (official National Commission for Culture and the Arts account).
- **Juan Tamad** is a recurring emblem of comic laziness, often represented by waiting under a guava tree. Multiple versions and retellings differ in details. https://folklore.usc.edu/the-legend-of-juan-tamad/ ; a later cultural production adapts it but adds a specific new cast https://culturalcenter.gov.ph/press-release/alice-reyes-dance-philippines-unveils-mga-kuwento-ni-juan-tamad-a-new-dance-adventure-for-young-audiences/.

## Relevant recorded variants and relationship patterns

| Traditional collection example | Juan / Pedro relationship in that version | What it does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|
| Fansler, **Juan the Peerless Robber** (told by Vicente M. Hilario from Batangas tradition) | Juan and Pedro as an initially allied duo of thieves; Juan is cunning, Pedro comparatively straightforward; the tale becomes violent, with Juan betraying Pedro | Not evidence that all Juan–Pedro jokes are criminal stories, or that Pedro canonically dies in every version |
| Fansler, **Lucas the Strong** | Juan and Pedro are **brothers** and both hardworking, contrasting with lazy Lucas | Cannot merge this Juan with the idle guava-tree Juan without authorial adaptation |
| Fansler, **Pablo and the Princess** | Juan, Pedro, Pablo are **three friends** who go seeking fortune | Does not create a stable Pablo shared with every Juan tale |
| Fansler, **Legend of Prince Oswaldo** | Juan, Pedro and Diego appear as companions in one plot, with magical objects | Does not imply Pedro has one recurring occupation or personality across stories |
| Fansler, **The Three Humpbacks** | Pedro and Juan are brothers of Pablo in a separate darkly comic plot | Not suitable as literal family canon for a friendly ongoing series |
| Fansler, **Pedro and Satan** / **Pedro and the Witch** | Pedro is himself protagonist of separate trickster/supernatural stories | Not necessarily the Pedro who travels with Juan in other tales |
| Fansler, **Juan and Maria**, **Juan the Fool**, **Juan and His Six Companions**, **Juan the Poor**, **Juan Sadut**, **Juan and his Painted Hat**, **The Monkey and Juan Pusong Tambi-Tambi** | Many independently told Juan-centered narratives including romance, wit, adventure and trickery | The presence of a Juan does not guarantee a common universe |
| Cole, **Juan Gathers Guavas** / **The Adventures of Juan** | Juan participates in another compilation's humorous/adventure material | Not evidence the same individual meets Pedro there |
| Modern CCP/ARDP, **Mga Kuwento ni Juan Tamad** (2024) | Invents a coherent stage adaptation with Juan, Matsing, Maria Masipag and animal characters | This **production's characters and quest** are not inherited automatic folklore canon; do not copy its new plot or distinctive ensemble |

## Story world choice and ethics

Two very different literary genres coexist in the source traditions: clever trickster tales, sometimes crude or violent, and moral family folk stories about idleness, ingenuity and daily community life. We deliberately choose a **family-safe, locally grounded serial** where Juan's slow/observant habit and Pedro's effort complement each other. We do **not** retell Fansler's fatal Pedro-and-Juan thief plot verbatim, treat NCCA regional names as separate physical characters, or copy CCP's 2024 created story, soundtrack, dance or character depictions.

**Our series-specific cast (all newly interpreted/created):** Juan, Pedro, Maria (librarian apprentice), Aling Sela, Kapitana Pilar, Mang Tino, Don Esteban, Nena, Elias, Aling Rosa. Some personal names are common Filipino names and appear in unrelated folktales, but their specific roles, relationships and plot in Maligaya are original to this project. Do not call them ethnographically attested counterparts.
